It's coming, and it's coming soon

Google made it official: AI Overviews and AI Mode will roll out in France by September 23, 2026 [F1]. If you use Google regularly — for research, to understand a topic, to find a service provider — your search experience is about to change. Not in six years. In a few weeks.

Why is France so late to the party? In more than 120 countries, AI Overviews have been available since mid-2024. France's delay comes down to specific regulatory constraints: neighboring rights for press publishers and the Digital Markets Act (DMA), the EU's regulation on digital markets [F3]. Google had to negotiate. Among other things, it offered French press publishers the right to opt their content out of these new features, transparency on generated impressions, and compensation through neighboring rights [F4]. Those discussions took time. That's why you haven't seen these summaries pop up yet when you search on google.fr.

This regulatory context isn't just a technical footnote. It says something important: the content you create or consume has value that platforms now have to acknowledge. That's genuinely good news in the long run, even if the transition is going to shake things up.

Two new interfaces, two different uses

AI Overviews and AI Mode are two distinct things, even though both are powered by Gemini, Google's AI model [F2].

AI Overviews are what you'll see first. When you type a question into Google, a box appears at the top of the page with an automatically generated summary. That summary comes with links to the sources used to build it. You no longer need to click through multiple results to get a quick answer — Google serves it up directly.

AI Mode is different. It offers a conversational interface: you ask a question, you get an answer, you can refine it, follow up, dig deeper. It's closer to chatting with an assistant than doing a classic keyword search. If you've ever used ChatGPT or Claude to ask questions, you get the idea — except here it's built right into Google.

Both features change one fundamental thing: search is no longer a list of links to scroll through. It's a direct answer, synthesized and presented before you've clicked on anything.

My takeaways

AI Mode doesn't replace classic search — it sits alongside it. For now, both coexist. But if you want to learn how to phrase effective queries in this kind of conversational interface, I wrote a practical article on how to write a good prompt when you're not a developer.

What this does to your traffic

Here's the number that stings: according to a Seer Interactive study covering more than 25 million impressions between June 2024 and September 2025, organic click-through rates drop by 61% when an AI summary appears in the results [S1]. In other words, when Google answers directly, people click through to sites a lot less.

This fits into a broader trend. More than 58% of Google searches now end with zero clicks, and that number keeps climbing as AI Overviews expand [S2]. These are called "zero-click searches": the user gets their answer without ever leaving Google.

What does that mean for you as an entrepreneur, concretely? If part of your traffic comes from informational searches — people trying to understand a topic in your field — that traffic is at risk of shrinking. Transactional searches (finding a service provider, comparing offers, booking an appointment) are less affected, because Google can't "convert" on your behalf yet. But blog posts, guides, explainer pages — those are going to face competition from AI summaries.

There's another data point worth keeping in mind. In March 2026, generative AI platforms were driving more than one billion referral visits per month, with AI-driven traffic growing 527% in one year [S3]. It's not just Google that's changing: it's the entire way people find information. Tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity also send traffic to websites — sometimes to yours, if your content gets cited.

Staying visible: what actually matters

When faced with these changes, the temptation is to hunt for a magic SEO trick. There isn't one. What determines whether your content gets cited in AI Overviews is the perceived quality of that content according to Google's E-E-A-T criteria [F5]:

  • Experience: you've lived what you're writing about.
  • Expertise: you know your subject inside out.
  • Authoritativeness: other credible sources cite or recognize you.
  • Trustworthiness: your site is transparent, your information is verifiable.

This framework has existed in Google's guidelines for years, but it takes on new weight when it's an AI deciding which sources deserve to be summarized and cited. Gemini isn't going to cite an anonymous, undated, authorless article on a site with no track record. It's going to look for content that shows there's a real person behind it with real experience.

A few concrete things you can do:

  • Sign your content: name, photo, short bio. Show who you are.
  • Date your articles and keep them updated. A piece from 2019 with no revisions sends a bad signal.
  • Cite your sources with links. If you're drawing on a study, a stat, a report — say so and link to it.
  • Answer a real question rather than producing content "for SEO." AI summaries cite pages that precisely match a search intent.
  • Structure your content with clear headings, lists, and direct answers. AI Overviews often pull from well-defined passages.

The trap to avoid

Churning out AI-generated content in bulk to "cover more topics" is probably the worst strategy in this context. Google can recognize that kind of content. And even if it doesn't penalize it directly, it won't cite it in its summaries either — it won't find the human experience its E-E-A-T criteria are looking for.

Fact-checking what Google tells you

AI Overviews are convenient. They're also fallible. AI models — including Gemini — can produce summaries that look reliable but contain errors, approximations, or information taken out of context. That's what's called a hallucination: the model generates something plausible that isn't actually true.

That's not a reason to avoid these tools. It's a reason to keep one simple habit: go back to the source. AI Overviews display links to the content used to build the summary [F2]. Click them. Read the original. Check that the summary actually reflects what the source says.

For anything important — a business decision, legal or tax information, a statistic you're going to use — never stop at the AI summary. It's a starting point, not a conclusion.

When Google isn't enough

Google is still where most searches start. But it's no longer the only relevant tool for your research or for finding reliable information.

A few alternatives worth knowing:

Perplexity: a conversational search engine that systematically cites its sources. Useful when you want a quick answer with full traceability.

ChatGPT (GPT-5, launched late June 2026 [F6]) and Claude (Fable 5 and Mythos 5, launched early June 2026 [F8]): AI assistants capable of synthesizing information, analyzing documents, and helping you frame complex questions. They don't replace search, but they can help you prepare or sharpen your queries.

Gemini (version 3.5 Flash, available since early July 2026 [F7]): Google's model, accessible directly at gemini.google.com, outside the classic search engine.

Mistral (Mistral Large 3.0, launched December 2025 [F10]): the French model — worth considering if you're working on sensitive topics or prefer a European alternative.

FAQ

When will AI Overviews and AI Mode be available to all users in France?

Google announced a rollout in France by September 23, 2026 [F1]. The exact timing for each user will depend on Google's phased deployment schedule, but that's the official deadline.

Will my site disappear from search results when Google's AI arrives?

No. Classic organic results continue to exist below AI Overviews. That said, traffic to certain types of informational content may decrease, since Google now answers some questions directly. The 61% click-through rate drop measured by Seer Interactive [S1] applies to queries where an AI summary appears — not to all searches.

How can I make sure my content gets cited in AI Overviews?

There's no guarantee. But the E-E-A-T criteria [F5] are your best lever: content that's signed, dated, sourced, structured, and precisely answers a real question. Google cites pages it considers reliable and relevant — not the most technically optimized ones.

Does AI Mode replace classic Google search?

No, not right now. Both coexist. AI Mode is an optional conversational interface [F2], not a replacement for keyword search. You can keep using Google exactly as you do today.

What are the risks of hallucinations or incorrect information in AI Overviews?

AI models can generate summaries that sound plausible but are inaccurate — that's what's called a hallucination. AI Overviews include links to their sources [F2]: get into the habit of checking them for anything important, rather than relying solely on the summary.

How do I measure the impact of AI Overviews on my site's traffic?

Google Search Console is still the go-to tool. You can track how impressions and clicks evolve by query. A drop in clicks on informational queries without a drop in impressions is often a sign that AI Overviews are capturing attention in your place.

My takeaways

The real shift isn't technical. It's a change in mindset. For years, producing content for Google meant optimizing for indexing bots. Now you also have to convince an AI model that your content deserves to be cited — and that comes down to the same thing as convincing a human: be clear, be credible, show where your information comes from.

For your research habits, the reflex to keep is simple: an AI summary is a starting point, never a conclusion. Always go back to the source before trusting information that matters.
