Design without a designer — is it actually possible?

When I launched my first side project, I spent two weeks going back and forth between hiring a freelance designer and cobbling something together myself in Canva. The DIY result was decent, but something was missing — that visual consistency that makes you recognize a brand at a glance.

Today, things have changed. Generative AI tools have made capabilities accessible that used to be reserved for professionals. And the numbers back it up: in 2025, 64% of marketers were using AI-based design tools every week, up from just 21% in 2023. In France, 31% of small businesses had adopted generative AI by February 2025 — a figure that had doubled in a year.

This isn't a trend. It's a fundamental shift in how marketing visuals get made.

But heads up: "accessible" doesn't mean "magic." AI doesn't read your mind. It produces what you ask for — and if you ask poorly, you get something generic. The good news is that getting it right is something you can learn fast.

In this article, I'll walk you through the concrete tools available today, the method for getting good results, and the limits you need to know before you publish anything.

The tools I wish I'd known about sooner

There are several families of AI tools for creating visuals today. I break them down by what you're trying to do.

For logos and visual identity

Canva Magic Studio is probably the most sensible starting point if you're coming in cold. Launched in 2026, it bundles five AI tools: Magic Design, Dream Lab, Magic Write, Canva Sheets, and Magic Charts. The advantage? You're working in an environment you might already know, with ready-to-use templates. Some features are available even on the free plan.

Adobe Firefly, integrated into Adobe Express, is another serious option. It received a wave of updates between late 2025 and mid-2026, including support for partner models like Gemini and Imagen. If you're already in the Adobe ecosystem, it's a natural extension.

For posters and flyers with text

This is where Ideogram really stands out. Most AI image generators have long had a major problem: text inside images came out unreadable, distorted, full of errors. Ideogram solved that — it generates readable text directly in the image, which makes it especially useful for flyers, promotional posters, or banners with a tagline.

For artistic visuals and high-end branding

Midjourney V6 delivers highly detailed outputs with greater control over style and lighting. It has a strong reputation for branding and artistic communication. It takes a bit more practice to master its parameters, but the results can be impressive.

To start for free

ChatGPT has integrated GPT Image 1 since March 2025, which replaced DALL-E 3 in the interface. DALL-E 3 itself remains freely accessible via Microsoft Copilot and Bing Image Creator. These are great entry points for testing without any financial commitment.

If you want to compare these tools based on your specific use case, I wrote a dedicated article on how to choose the right AI for your needs.

Where to start

If you've never used an AI image generator, start with Canva Magic Studio or Bing Image Creator. Free, in English, no brutal learning curve. You can always move to more powerful tools once you've got the hang of how prompting works.

From idea to downloadable file

Here's how I actually go about creating a visual for a project.

Step 1: define your intent before opening any tool

Before I type a single word, I ask myself three questions: who is this visual for? Where will it be used (social media, print, website)? What feeling do I want to convey?

A logo for an artisan bakery doesn't speak the same visual language as a logo for a consulting firm. AI doesn't know that unless you tell it.

Step 2: write a precise prompt

This is the step that makes all the difference. A vague prompt like "logo for my bakery" gives you something generic. A prompt like "minimalist logo for an artisan bakery, 1950s vintage style, warm terracotta and cream colors, no text, square format" gives you something you can actually work with.

I laid out the full method in this article: how to write a good prompt when you're not a developer. And if you want to understand the theory behind it, the prompt engineering glossary entry is a good complement.

Step 3: iterate, don't start over

AI rarely nails it on the first try. That's normal. The right approach is to adjust your prompt gradually: change one word, specify a color, ask for a darker or more minimal variation. After three or four iterations, you usually land on something solid.

Step 4: finish in Canva or Adobe Express

Most AI image generators produce an image — not an editable design file. To add your name, tweak a color, or adapt the format from an Instagram story to an A4 poster, you then move into Canva or Adobe Express. That's where the two worlds combine well.

Writing a prompt that actually delivers

I'll be straight with you: the quality of your prompt is 80% of the result.

A good prompt for a marketing visual typically includes:

  • The type of visual: logo, poster, flyer, banner, illustration…
  • The style: minimalist, illustrated, photographic, vintage, geometric…
  • The colors: be specific. "Blue" is vague. "Dark navy, almost black" is useful.
  • The mood or emotion: warm, professional, energetic, reassuring…
  • What you do NOT want: if you don't want text in the image, say so. If you don't want human faces, specify that.
  • The format or composition: centered, white background, square format…

For flyers with visible text, Ideogram is the way to go. For other generators, it's better to generate the image without text, then add the text in Canva.

The trap to avoid

Asking AI to "make something beautiful and professional" gets you nowhere. AI doesn't have taste — it has parameters. The more precisely you describe what you want, the closer the result is to what you had in mind. "Professional" means nothing to an image model.

Free vs. paid: what it actually changes

The budget question comes up a lot. Here's how I see it.

Free options are real. Bing Image Creator (DALL-E 3), Canva's free plan with Magic Studio, Ideogram with limited access — you can create decent visuals without spending a dime. For testing, validating a visual direction, or creating a first launch logo, that's enough.

Paid options mainly unlock two things: the number of generations and the resolution of exported files. If you need to produce visuals regularly — weekly flyers, daily posts — a Canva Pro or Midjourney subscription pays for itself pretty quickly compared to the time you'd spend working around free-tier limits.

I compared the free vs. paid logic in more detail in this article: free or paid AI — how do you choose?

One thing to keep in mind: free models often have restrictions on commercial use. Before using a generated image for an ad campaign or product packaging, check the tool's terms of service.

What you need to know before you publish

AI generates fast. Sometimes too fast. A few things to watch out for before putting a visual online or sending it to print.

Commercial use rights

Every tool has its own rules. ChatGPT with GPT Image 1, Canva Magic Studio, and Adobe Firefly generally allow commercial use of generated images — but read the terms of service, they evolve. Midjourney has different rules depending on your subscription tier.

Brand consistency

AI can generate beautiful visuals, but it doesn't know your brand identity. If you already have defined colors, typography, and a visual tone, you need to give those to the AI explicitly in every prompt. Otherwise, each generation will head off in a different direction.

Human review is still essential

Image generators sometimes produce subtle anomalies: a slightly malformed number, a barely broken symmetry, a strange detail in a corner. Take the time to look at the full image before signing off on it.

Uniqueness isn't guaranteed

A generic prompt can produce images similar to what others have generated with the same words. For a logo, that's a real concern. The more specific your prompt is to your own universe, the less likely you are to end up with something that feels "seen before."

FAQ

Can AI really create a professional, unique logo for your brand?

Yes, with some nuance. AI can generate visually solid logos that work well for a small business. But uniqueness depends on how precise your prompt is: the more specifically you describe your universe, the less likely you are to end up with something generic. For a logo you plan to trademark, it's better to work with a designer who can guarantee originality and file scalability.

What are the best free tools for designing posters and flyers?

Canva Magic Studio (free plan), Bing Image Creator powered by DALL-E 3, and Ideogram all offer free access. Canva is particularly well-suited for flyers because you can combine image generation and layout in the same tool. Go with Ideogram if you need readable text embedded directly in the image.

How do you make sure AI-generated images are cleared for commercial use?

Read each tool's terms of service before any commercial publication. Adobe Firefly and Canva Magic Studio are known for their commercial-friendly approach, with models trained on licensed content. For Midjourney, rights vary by subscription tier. When in doubt, contact the tool's support team or stick to platforms whose terms of service explicitly allow commercial use.

Do you need technical or artistic skills to use these generators?

No. These tools are designed for non-specialists. The only skill worth developing is writing clear prompts — and that's something you can pick up in a few hours of practice. You don't need to know how to draw, or understand typography or graphic composition, to get good results.

How do you write an effective prompt to get the exact design you have in mind?

Describe the type of visual, the style, the precise colors, the mood, the composition, and what you don't want. The more specific you are, the closer the result is to your intention. Iterate: adjust one element at a time rather than rewriting everything. I laid out this method in detail in the article how to write a good prompt.

Can AI tools help you create visuals that are consistent with your existing brand identity?

Yes, if you give them the right information. Share your exact colors (hex codes if possible), your visual style, your reference typography, and examples of visuals you like. Canva Magic Studio also lets you create a brand kit that the AI tools respect when generating. Consistency doesn't come automatically — it comes from a precise description repeated in every prompt.

My takeaways

AI doesn't replace a good designer for a complex brand project. But for an entrepreneur who needs a flyer for an event next week, a poster for their storefront, or a logo to test a new service — it genuinely changes the game.

What I've learned from testing these tools: the time you invest in writing your prompt is directly proportional to the quality of the result. It's not about technical skill. It's about being clear on what you want.
