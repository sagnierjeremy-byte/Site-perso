LLM

An LLM (large language model) is the brain that powers AIs like ChatGPT or Claude: a program trained on mountains of text that guesses the most likely next word, one after another, to understand your question and answer it in plain language.

— Definition

LLM, in plain words

In plain words: it's a program that has digested a gigantic amount of text, and uses that to guess — word after word — the most likely continuation of what you write.

Think of it as a language engine, not a typewriter: it doesn't copy anything, it recomposes an answer every time, from what it has learned.

A concrete example

When you ask Claude to summarize an article, the LLM reads the text, predicts a plausible answer, and adjusts it to the context.

Why it matters

Almost every other word in the glossary starts here: prompt, token, context, hallucination, RAG.

It's hiding behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and a lot of assistants built into your tools.

Don't mix it up with

AI model: An AI model is the engine that reads your request and produces an answer.

Token: A token is a small chunk of text the AI counts to measure what it reads, writes, and bills.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking an LLM always knows where its information comes from.
  • Treating it like a perfect database.
  • Forgetting it can be convincing and wrong.

Quick questions

What is an LLM in AI?

An LLM (large language model) is the program trained on huge amounts of text that powers AIs like ChatGPT or Claude: it understands your request and generates an answer in plain language.

Where will I run into LLMs?

They're hiding behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and many assistants built into your tools.
